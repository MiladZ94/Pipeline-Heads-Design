Pipeline Installation Heads — Operational Overview & Guideline Mapping

Technical fundamentals, operational head types (A, B, C), functional necessity, and direct mapping to the 5 Guideline Phases.
What is an Installation Head?
A subsea pipeline installation head is a heavy-duty, engineered structural header welded or flanged to the pipeline end during offshore installation (S-Lay, J-Lay, Reel-Lay). It serves as the primary mechanical and pressure interface between the vessel A&R winch and the submarine pipeline.
Why are Installation Heads Used? (4 Key Functions)
1. Tension Load Transfer: Safely transfers A&R winch pull forces ($F_d$) into the pipe wall.
2. Hydrostatic & Pressure Seal: Keeps seawater out during lay; withstands hydrotest ($P_{hydro}$).
3. Pigging & Dewatering Interface: Houses closure plates, threadolets & pig stoppers for flooding/purging.
4. Seabed Handling & Protection: Enables subsea initiation, abandonment & roller protection via shroud.

Operational Head Types (A, B, C)

HEAD A — Start-up / Laydown (SU/LD)
Pipeline Initiation & Endpoint Laydown
Welded/flanged to pipeline end for launching off the vessel stinger and laying down on seabed. Includes roller protective shroud & padeyes.
HEAD B — Abandonment & Recovery (A&R)
Storm & Weather Abandonment
Attached when lay operations are paused due to severe sea states. Lowered to seabed via A&R cable and recovered when weather clears.
HEAD C — Wet-Buckle Recovery (WBR)
Emergency Subsea Pipe Recovery
Inserted subsea into a flooded/buckled pipeline to seal the pipe, dewater/purge seawater with gas, and pull the pipeline back to vessel.

Lessons — The 10 Design Checks

Each lesson: what it protects against · formula · variable meaning · worked example · acceptance.
1
Bearing / Contact Stress
DNV-ST-N001 §P2.7
Purpose: prevents localized yielding where the shackle pin presses against padeye hole.
Formula — concentrated contact ($D_p/D_h < 0.94$)
$$\frac{\sigma_y}{\gamma_m} \ge 0.18\sqrt{F_d \left(\frac{1}{D_p}-\frac{1}{D_h}\right)\frac{E\beta}{t}}$$
Variables: $F_d$ design load · $D_p$ pin Ø · $D_h$ hole Ø · $E$ modulus · $t$ thickness · $\gamma_m = 1.15$
Example (8" A-head): 262 MPa ≤ 300 → UC = 0.87 PASS
2
Tear-Out (Shear-Out) Resistance
DNV-ST-N001
Purpose: prevents pin from shearing through material between hole and plate edge.
Formula
$$\frac{\sigma_y}{\gamma_m} \ge \frac{1.7\, F_d}{(2 R_{pad} - D_h)\, t}$$
Variables: $R_{pad}$ padeye radius · $D_h$ hole Ø · $t$ thickness · $1.7$ empirical factor
Example: 82 MPa ≤ 300 → UC = 0.27 PASS
3
Cheek Plate Welds
DNV-ST-N001
Purpose: verifies fillet weld joining cheek plates to main plate.
Formula
$$\frac{\sigma_y}{\gamma_{mw}} \ge \frac{F_d\, t_{ch}\, \delta}{1.5\, t\, D_{ch}\, a} \qquad \delta = \frac{4\tan(v)\,h}{t}+1$$
Variables: $t_{ch}$ cheek thickness · $D_{ch}$ cheek Ø · $a$ throat · $\gamma_{mw}=1.30$
Example: 114 MPa ≤ 265 → UC = 0.43 PASS
4
Main Plate → Pipe Weld
DNV-ST-N001 · directional
Purpose: critical weld between padeye main plate and pipe under $F_d$ + moments.
Formula — von Mises method
$$\sqrt{\sigma_\perp^2 + 3\left(\tau_\perp^2 + \tau_\parallel^2\right)} \le \frac{f_u}{\gamma_{mw}}$$
Variables: $\sigma_\perp$ normal stress · $\tau_\perp,\ \tau_\parallel$ shear · $f_u$ ultimate · $\gamma_{mw}=1.30$
Example: 132 MPa ≤ 346 → UC = 0.38 PASS
5
Sectional Verifications (0-0 … 3-3)
EN 1993-1-1 §6.2
Purpose: combined axial + bending + shear at critical cross-sections.
Formula
$$\frac{\sigma_y}{\gamma_m} \ge \sqrt{(\sigma_{axial}+\sigma_{bend})^2 + 3\,\tau_{shear}^2}$$
Variables: $\sigma_{axial}$ from $F_d$ · $\sigma_{bend}$ from moments · $\tau_{shear}$ transverse · $\gamma_m=1.15$
Example (section 3-3): 225 MPa ≤ 300 → UC = 0.75 PASS
6
Closure Plate Bending
Roark Table 11.2
Purpose: solid semicircular plate at pipe end loaded by hydrotest pressure.
Formula — radial bending stresses
$$\sigma_A = -0.42\, P_{LD}\frac{a^2}{t^2} \qquad \sigma_B = -0.36\, P_{LD}\frac{a^2}{t^2} \qquad \sigma_C = +0.21\, P_{LD}\frac{a^2}{t^2}$$
Variables: $a$ plate radius · $t$ thickness · $P_{hydro}$ pressure · $\gamma_Q=1.50$, $\gamma_m=1.15$
Example: max 66 MPa ≤ 300 → UC = 0.22 PASS
7
Pig Stopper
Design
Purpose: stops pig from exiting pipe end during flooding/gauging.
Formula
$$\sigma = \frac{F_d}{A_{effective}} \le \frac{\sigma_y}{\gamma_m}$$
Example: 109 MPa ≤ 291 → UC = 0.37 PASS
8
Threadolet Weld
EC3
Purpose: weld of vent/pigging threadolet under hydrotest pressure.
Formula
$$\frac{P\, \gamma_Q\, A_{hole}}{A_{weld}} \le \frac{\sigma_u}{0.9\,\gamma_{mw}\sqrt{3}}$$
Example: 17 MPa ≤ 232 → UC = 0.07 PASS
9
Lifting Padeye
DNV-N001 · DAF 2.44
Purpose: handles head weight during handling.
Design load & checks
$$F_d = \gamma_Q \cdot DAF \cdot W_{max} \qquad f_p = \frac{F_d}{D_{ph}\, t} \le \frac{\sigma_y}{\gamma_m}$$
Example: bearing 192 ≤ 300 (0.64) · shear-out 96 ≤ 173 (0.55) — PASS
10
Shroud (Start-up / Laydown)
DNV-N001 + EC3
Purpose: protects head on roller during lay; checks bending + clamp bolts.
Bending & bolts
$$M_{max}=\gamma_Q R_v\frac{L}{4} \qquad \sigma_{bend}=\frac{M_{max}}{W} \le \frac{\sigma_y}{\gamma_m}$$
Example: M24×8 bolts, bending 36 ≤ 309 → UC = 0.12 PASS

PHASE 1 — Material Selection & Property Matrix Material Decision Table
Select materials matching line pipe grade, structural standards, and offshore subsea corrosion criteria:

Component Selection Rules:

  • Line Pipe Header: API 5L X52 / X60 / X65 / X70 (must match or exceed pipeline grade).
  • Structural Plates & Padeyes: EN 10025 S355J0 or S355J2 (use J2 for subzero design temperatures).
  • Forged Flanges: ASTM A694 F52 / F60 / F65 (matching line pipe yield strength).
  • Fasteners: ASTM A193 Grade B7 stud bolts + ASTM A194 2H nuts (PTFE coated for subsea corrosion protection).
  • Gaskets: KaMOS RTJ Octagonal Stainless Steel SS316 Ring Joint Gasket.

Critical Material Property Rules:

S355 Plate Yield Strength vs Thickness ($t$) — EN 10025:
$$\sigma_y = 355\text{ MPa } (t \le 16\text{ mm}) \quad \mid \quad \sigma_y = 345\text{ MPa } (16 < t \le 40\text{ mm}) \quad \mid \quad \sigma_y = 335\text{ MPa } (40 < t \le 63\text{ mm})$$
Elastic Constants: Young's Modulus $E = 210,000\text{ MPa}$, Poisson's ratio $\nu = 0.3$, Density $\rho = 7,850\text{ kg/m}^3$.
PHASE 2 — Design Load Calculation Workflow (DNV LRFD) Load Factoring
Calculate the factored LRFD design pull force ($F_d$) and decompose into 3D load components:
Step 1 — Static/Dynamic Pull & LRFD Factoring (DNV-ST-N001 §P.2.6):
$$F_{SD} = \max(F_{nom} \cdot DAF,\ F_{DL}) \qquad \longrightarrow \qquad F_d = F_{SD} \cdot \gamma_c \cdot \gamma_f = F_{SD} \cdot 1.30 \cdot 1.30 = 1.69 \cdot F_{SD}$$
  • Consequence Factor ($\gamma_c$): $1.30$ (DNV-ST-N001 Table 16-1 for major installation equipment).
  • Load Factor ($\gamma_f$): $1.30$ (DNV-ST-N001 LRFD limit-state combination).
  • DAF (Dynamic Factor): $\approx 1.40$ for winching/pipelay; $2.44$ for deck lifting padeye.
  • 5° + 5° Load Vector Decomposition: Apply $5^\circ$ in-plane + $5^\circ$ out-of-plane angles to padeye pin: $$F_{axial} = F_d \cdot \cos(5^\circ) \cdot \cos(5^\circ) \approx 0.992 \cdot F_d$$ $$F_{in-plane} = F_d \cdot \sin(5^\circ) \cdot \cos(5^\circ) \approx 0.087 \cdot F_d$$ $$F_{out-of-plane} = F_d \cdot \sin(5^\circ) \approx 0.087 \cdot F_d$$
PHASE 3 — Geometry & Component Sizing Rules of Thumb Initial Sizing
Establish initial physical dimensions before running formal structural verifications:

1. Main Padeye & Pin Sizing:

  • Shackle Selection: Choose Shackle WLL $\ge F_{SD}$ from Wide Body catalog (e.g. 55t Wide Body P-6013 with $D_{pin} = 57\text{ mm}$).
  • Hole Diameter ($D_{hole}$): $D_{hole} = D_{pin} + \text{GAP}$, where $\text{GAP} \ge \max(2\text{ mm},\ 3\% D_{pin})$. (Crucial to prevent pin binding!)
  • Main Plate Thickness ($t_{main}$): First estimate $t_{main} \approx \dfrac{F_d}{\sigma_{allow} \cdot D_{pin}}$.
  • Outer Radius ($R_{pad}$): $R_{pad} \approx 1.5 \cdot D_{hole}$.
  • Cheek Plates ($t_{cheek}$): $t_{cheek} \approx 0.5 \cdot t_{main}$ (welded both sides).

2. Closure Plate & Shroud Sizing:

  • Closure Plate ($t_{closure}$): Semicircular solid plate thickness sized by Roark Table 11.2 formula under hydrotest pressure $P_{hydro} \cdot \gamma_Q$ ($\gamma_Q = 1.50$).
  • Shroud Protection: Rolled shell thickness $t_{shroud} \approx 10\text{--}12\text{ mm}$, clamp bolts M24 Grade 8.8 pretensioned to $70\% f_{ub} A_s$.
  • Threadolets & Nipples: 2" 3000# or 6000# NPT threadolets welded to pipe for vent/drain.
PHASE 4 — Execution & Acceptance Criteria for the 10 Checks Structural Verification
Run all 10 structural & pressure checks. Every single check must satisfy $\text{Unity Check (UC)} \le 1.0$:
Acceptance Criterion Rule:
$$\text{Unity Check (UC)} = \frac{\text{Calculated Stress } (\sigma_{calc})}{\text{Allowable Stress Limit } (\sigma_{allow})} \le 1.00 \qquad \text{where } \sigma_{allow} = \frac{\sigma_y}{\gamma_m} \text{ or } \frac{f_u}{\gamma_{mw}}$$

Assemble the final engineering documentation for Marine Warranty Surveyor (MWS) and client approval:

Engineering Pitfalls & Technical Solutions Matrix

#Common Engineering PitfallRoot Cause / RiskMandatory Technical Fix
1Omitting 5° Out-of-Plane AngleIgnoring transverse bending moment causes premature weld/section yieldingDecompose $F_d$ into $F_{axial}$, $F_{in-plane}$ & $F_{out-of-plane}$ components
2Using Wrong DAFUsing tension DAF ($\approx 1.4$) for deck lifting causes padeye failureApply $\text{DAF} = 2.44$ for deck lifting padeye per DNV-ST-N001 Table 16-1
3Confusing Weld & Material Safety FactorsOverestimating weld allowable by using $\gamma_m=1.15$ instead of $\gamma_{mw}=1.30$Strictly apply $\gamma_{mw} = 1.30$ for all structural weld checks
4Pin Gap Clearance Too TightGAP $< 2\text{ mm}$ causes shackle pin corner pinching & high stress concentrationsEnsure $\text{GAP} = D_{hole} - D_{pin} \ge \max(2\text{ mm},\ 3\% D_{pin})$
5Inconsistent Unit SystemsMixing $\text{kN}$ with $\text{N}$, or $\text{mm}$ with $\text{m}$ (1000× calculation error)Convert all forces to Newtons [$\text{N}$] and dimensions to millimeters [$\text{mm}$] ($\text{MPa} = \text{N/mm}^2$)
6Ignoring S355 Yield Drop with ThicknessAssuming $\sigma_y = 355\text{ MPa}$ for $t = 40\text{ mm}$ plate ($\sigma_y$ drops to $345\text{--}335\text{ MPa}$)Apply EN 10025 thickness table: $355\text{ MPa } (\le 16), 345\text{ MPa } (16\text{--}40), 335\text{ MPa } (40\text{--}63)$
7Only Verifying Section 3-3Assuming padeye base (Section 3-3) is always worst, ignoring Section 0-0/1-1/2-2Calculate and report combined stresses at ALL cross-sections 0-0 through 3-3
8Missing Hertz Contact Check (Head C)High Hertz contact stress between recovery pin and pipe inner wall causes local dentingRun Hertzian contact stress check for Head C ($\sigma_{Hertz} \le 0.90 \sigma_y$)
