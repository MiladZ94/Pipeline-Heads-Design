Pipeline Installation Heads — Operational Overview & Guideline Mapping

Technical fundamentals, operational head types (A, B, C), functional necessity, and direct mapping to the 5 Guideline Phases.
What is an Installation Head?
A subsea pipeline installation head is a heavy-duty, engineered structural header welded or flanged to the pipeline end during offshore installation (S-Lay, J-Lay, Reel-Lay). It serves as the primary mechanical and pressure interface between the vessel A&R winch and the submarine pipeline.
Why are Installation Heads Used? (4 Key Functions)
1. Tension Load Transfer: Safely transfers A&R winch pull forces ($F_d$) into the pipe wall.
2. Hydrostatic & Pressure Seal: Keeps seawater out during lay; withstands hydrotest ($P_{hydro}$).
3. Pigging & Dewatering Interface: Houses closure plates, threadolets & pig stoppers for flooding/purging.
4. Seabed Handling & Protection: Enables subsea initiation, abandonment & roller protection via shroud.

Operational Head Types (A, B, C)

HEAD A — Start-up / Laydown (SU/LD)
Pipeline Initiation & Endpoint Laydown
Welded/flanged to pipeline end for launching off the vessel stinger and laying down on seabed. Includes roller protective shroud & padeyes.
HEAD B — Abandonment & Recovery (A&R)
Storm & Weather Abandonment
Attached when lay operations are paused due to severe sea states. Lowered to seabed via A&R cable and recovered when weather clears.
HEAD C — Wet-Buckle Recovery (WBR)
Emergency Subsea Pipe Recovery
Inserted subsea into a flooded/buckled pipeline to seal the pipe, dewater/purge seawater with gas, and pull the pipeline back to vessel.

Lessons — The 10 Design Checks

Each lesson: what it protects against · formula · variable meaning · worked example · acceptance.
1
Bearing / Contact Stress
DNV-ST-N001 §P2.7
Purpose: prevents localized yielding where the shackle pin presses against padeye hole.
Formula — concentrated contact ($D_p/D_h < 0.94$)
$$\frac{\sigma_y}{\gamma_m} \ge 0.18\sqrt{F_d \left(\frac{1}{D_p}-\frac{1}{D_h}\right)\frac{E\beta}{t}}$$
Variables: $F_d$ design load · $D_p$ pin Ø · $D_h$ hole Ø · $E$ modulus · $t$ thickness · $\gamma_m = 1.15$
Example (8" A-head): 262 MPa ≤ 300 → UC = 0.87 PASS
2
Tear-Out (Shear-Out) Resistance
DNV-ST-N001
Purpose: prevents pin from shearing through material between hole and plate edge.
Formula
$$\frac{\sigma_y}{\gamma_m} \ge \frac{1.7\, F_d}{(2 R_{pad} - D_h)\, t}$$
Variables: $R_{pad}$ padeye radius · $D_h$ hole Ø · $t$ thickness · $1.7$ empirical factor
Example: 82 MPa ≤ 300 → UC = 0.27 PASS
3
Cheek Plate Welds
DNV-ST-N001
Purpose: verifies fillet weld joining cheek plates to main plate.
Formula
$$\frac{\sigma_y}{\gamma_{mw}} \ge \frac{F_d\, t_{ch}\, \delta}{1.5\, t\, D_{ch}\, a} \qquad \delta = \frac{4\tan(v)\,h}{t}+1$$
Variables: $t_{ch}$ cheek thickness · $D_{ch}$ cheek Ø · $a$ throat · $\gamma_{mw}=1.30$
Example: 114 MPa ≤ 265 → UC = 0.43 PASS
4
Main Plate → Pipe Weld
DNV-ST-N001 · directional
Purpose: critical weld between padeye main plate and pipe under $F_d$ + moments.
Formula — von Mises method
$$\sqrt{\sigma_\perp^2 + 3\left(\tau_\perp^2 + \tau_\parallel^2\right)} \le \frac{f_u}{\gamma_{mw}}$$
Variables: $\sigma_\perp$ normal stress · $\tau_\perp,\ \tau_\parallel$ shear · $f_u$ ultimate · $\gamma_{mw}=1.30$
Example: 132 MPa ≤ 346 → UC = 0.38 PASS
5
Sectional Verifications (0-0 … 3-3)
EN 1993-1-1 §6.2
Purpose: combined axial + bending + shear at critical cross-sections.
Formula
$$\frac{\sigma_y}{\gamma_m} \ge \sqrt{(\sigma_{axial}+\sigma_{bend})^2 + 3\,\tau_{shear}^2}$$
Variables: $\sigma_{axial}$ from $F_d$ · $\sigma_{bend}$ from moments · $\tau_{shear}$ transverse · $\gamma_m=1.15$
Example (section 3-3): 225 MPa ≤ 300 → UC = 0.75 PASS
6
Closure Plate Bending
Roark Table 11.2
Purpose: solid semicircular plate at pipe end loaded by hydrotest pressure.
Formula — radial bending stresses
$$\sigma_A = -0.42\, P_{LD}\frac{a^2}{t^2} \qquad \sigma_B = -0.36\, P_{LD}\frac{a^2}{t^2} \qquad \sigma_C = +0.21\, P_{LD}\frac{a^2}{t^2}$$
Variables: $a$ plate radius · $t$ thickness · $P_{hydro}$ pressure · $\gamma_Q=1.50$, $\gamma_m=1.15$
Example: max 66 MPa ≤ 300 → UC = 0.22 PASS
7
Pig Stopper
Design
Purpose: stops pig from exiting pipe end during flooding/gauging.
Formula
$$\sigma = \frac{F_d}{A_{effective}} \le \frac{\sigma_y}{\gamma_m}$$
Example: 109 MPa ≤ 291 → UC = 0.37 PASS
8
Threadolet Weld
EC3
Purpose: weld of vent/pigging threadolet under hydrotest pressure.
Formula
$$\frac{P\, \gamma_Q\, A_{hole}}{A_{weld}} \le \frac{\sigma_u}{0.9\,\gamma_{mw}\sqrt{3}}$$
Example: 17 MPa ≤ 232 → UC = 0.07 PASS
9
Lifting Padeye
DNV-N001 · DAF 2.44
Purpose: handles head weight during handling.
Design load & checks
$$F_d = \gamma_Q \cdot DAF \cdot W_{max} \qquad f_p = \frac{F_d}{D_{ph}\, t} \le \frac{\sigma_y}{\gamma_m}$$
Example: bearing 192 ≤ 300 (0.64) · shear-out 96 ≤ 173 (0.55) — PASS
10
Shroud (Start-up / Laydown)
DNV-N001 + EC3
Purpose: protects head on roller during lay; checks bending + clamp bolts.
Bending & bolts
$$M_{max}=\gamma_Q R_v\frac{L}{4} \qquad \sigma_{bend}=\frac{M_{max}}{W} \le \frac{\sigma_y}{\gamma_m}$$
Example: M24×8 bolts, bending 36 ≤ 309 → UC = 0.12 PASS

Step-by-Step Guideline

Phases of design and common engineering pitfalls.
PHASE 0 — Collect Inputs
  • DN [in], pipe grade, t_pipe
  • F_nom [kN], DAF, dynamic load F_DL
  • P_hydro [MPa], head weight W [t]
  • Head types (A: SU/LD · B: A&R · C: WBR), sling angles 5°+5°
PHASE 1 — Materials
  • Pipe → API 5L · Plates → S355J0/J2 · Flange → A694 F52/60/65
  • Bolts → A193 B7 PTFE · Nuts → A194 2H · Gasket → KaMOS RTJ
PHASE 2 — Design Load (LRFD)
$$F_{SD} = \max(F_{nom}\cdot DAF,\ F_{DL}) \quad \longrightarrow \quad F_d = F_{SD}\cdot\gamma_c\cdot\gamma_f$$
  • γc = 1.30, γf = 1.30, DAF ≈ 1.4 (DNV Table 16-1)
PHASE 3 — Preliminary Sizing
  • D_pin from shackle catalog → D_hole = D_pin + GAP
  • t_main ≈ F_d/(σ_allow·D_pin) · R_pad ≈ 1.5·D_hole
PHASE 4 — Run 10 Checks
  • Bearing → Tear-out → Cheek weld → Main weld → Sections → Closure → Pig stopper → Threadolet → Lifting → Shroud
PHASE 5 — Deliverables
  • Drawing: assembly + details + BOM
  • Report: Intro · Defs · Refs · Scope · Summary (UC table!) · Appendices
